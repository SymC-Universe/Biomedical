## Deep search B — native biological generators for chi_bio

Status: **completed**

Purpose:
- find biological systems whose native dynamics might legitimately license a scalar `χ_bio`;
- distinguish second-order/eigenmode-compatible cases from first-order/Markov/nonlinear systems;
- avoid importing a mechanical damping ratio by analogy.

Workspace path:
`/Native biological generators for chi_bio`

Primary output:
- 278 relevant papers surfaced;
- NF-kB, p53/Mdm2, and ERK/MAPK are the strongest opening generator families;
- HOG, calcium, circadian, and switching systems often demand other generator-aware coordinates or refusal.

High-priority papers read in full:
- Jaruszewicz-Błońska et al. 2023, PLOS ONE, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0286416.
- Son et al. 2021, Science Signaling, DOI 10.1126/scisignal.aaz4382.
- Geva-Zatorsky et al. 2006, Molecular Systems Biology, DOI 10.1038/msb4100068.
- Blum et al. 2019, Molecular Systems Biology, DOI 10.15252/msb.20198947.

Opening conclusion:
- an identifiable local complex mode is plausible in some biological generators;
- such a mode is not automatically a mechanical oscillator;
- p53 provides an important anti-shortcut example because population damping can arise from single-cell dephasing while individual oscillations remain sustained;
- NF-kB reduced models offer a stronger identifiability route but still require direct eigenmode/representation tests;
- ERK temporal perturbation is likely to be at least as informative at the modal/transfer-function level as at the scalar level.

## Targeted extraction rule

For every candidate promoted from literature to executable P0-D:
1. read the full paper;
2. extract exact perturbation and recovery design;
3. verify source accessions independently;
4. record native biological metric/model;
5. identify what the paper directly measures versus infers;
6. record whether `χ_bio`, `Χ_bio`, or Bio Chi is actually testable;
7. freeze source and representation before opening any new target outcome.
