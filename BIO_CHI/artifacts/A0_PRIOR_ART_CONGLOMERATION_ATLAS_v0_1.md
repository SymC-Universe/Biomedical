## Direct agreements already inherited

1. Biological systems can exhibit reversible state changes, recovery, memory, hysteresis and reorganization.
2. Perturbation history and structural state can alter later responses.
3. Biological generators are heterogeneous; equilibrium, limit-cycle, adaptive, switching and stochastic descriptions all occur.
4. Single-cell, population and reduced-model observables can support different dynamical interpretations.
5. Perturbation without recovery is insufficient for a recovery claim.
6. Reproducible source/method access can limit what is testable even when biology is strong.

## Direct disagreements / unresolved observations

- Some systems/regimes support identifiable complex modes; others do not.
- An observable may be oscillatory while the slowest or governing local mode is different.
- A local scalar may be unique in one regime, multiple in another, or unavailable in another.
- The relation between local modes and nonlinear realized recovery remains incompletely established.
- Substrate/carrier persistence is demonstrated in specific systems, but its relation to modal or recovery reorganization is not generally mapped.
- Cross-system evidence does not license one universal decomposition or scalar.

## Prior-art compatibility clusters

### Cluster A: Local-generator / modal qualification
Jaruszewicz NF-kB, selected p53 models, ERK network models and related dynamical systems can support local or transfer-function analysis when operating state, input baseline and observable are explicit.

### Cluster B: History-dependent system behavior
NF-kB memory, drug-tolerant states, chromatin-conditioned transitions and repeated-perturbation systems require history and may not be reducible to an equilibrium scalar.

### Cluster C: Substrate / carrier inheritance
Lineage, chromatin, methylation and persistent regulatory states can condition later behavior in specific systems, but carrier identity and causal intervention remain system-specific.

### Cluster D: Refusal / boundary systems
Multiple stable complex pairs, nondifferentiable local generators, limit cycles, switching systems, response-only datasets and source-method under-specification define where a proposed representation cannot be admitted.

## Conglomerated implications available without new data

1. **Conditional scalar principle:** any biological scalar stability coordinate must be conditioned on native model class, operating state, observable and identifiability.
2. **Layer-separation principle:** scalar adequacy, modal adequacy and full-system adequacy are distinct empirical questions.
3. **History principle:** recovery and memory require temporal history and cannot be inferred solely from one equilibrium.
4. **Carrier principle:** inheritance claims require a measured or manipulated carrier, not merely a persistent phenotype or metadata label.
5. **Refusal principle:** nonidentifiability, absent modes, nondifferentiability, incompatible design and source-method gaps are legitimate scientific map coordinates.
6. **Joint-testing implication:** informative next tests compare scalar, modal and system behavior within the same native system and operating-state definition before cross-system transport.

These are synthesis-level or candidate conglomerated inferences until prospectively tested where they make realized-behavior claims.

## A0 residual mapping target

The empirical Bio Chi Atlas should not map “biological stability exists” or “biology has memory” as new territory. Its residual target is:

> the **domains of validity and failure** of scalar, modal/component and system/conglomerate stability representations, their relationship under matched native conditions, and the conditions under which substrate/carrier state predicts a change in those relationships.
