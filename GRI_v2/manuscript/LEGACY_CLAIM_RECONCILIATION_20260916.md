# GRI legacy-claim reconciliation

**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** EDITORIAL/SCIENTIFIC CLAIM-CEILING CONTROL  
**Applies to:** revised oncology manuscript, figures, captions, supplement, repository reader-facing text

## Purpose

The revised paper should not inherit a stronger claim merely because an older figure, phrase, axis label, or manuscript sentence survived from the original GRI lineage. This ledger records the highest-risk legacy language and the conditions under which it is retired, relabeled, preserved as historical provenance, or could someday be reinstated by new evidence.

The rule is asymmetric: weaker, accurate wording may replace stronger unsupported wording mechanically; stronger wording requires new scientific support.

## Figure-language rule

A figure is part of the scientific claim. If the prose says `NOT_ADMITTED` but an axis, color band, legend, title, or annotation still says `Critical`, `Failure`, `Energy`, `Optimal`, or `Chi = 1` without an earned definition, the figure overrules the cautionary prose in the reader's perception and the paper is internally inconsistent.

Therefore every regenerated figure must pass the same claim-ceiling audit as the main text.

## Historical-provenance rule

Legacy wording may be retained in an explicitly historical appendix, correction narrative, or repository artifact when the purpose is to document what changed. In that setting it must be marked as superseded and must not appear as a current result.

Do not silently rewrite a frozen historical artifact merely because its interpretation is obsolete. Correct the current manuscript, caption, README, or successor record while preserving the historical source.

## Reinstatement rule

None of the retired terms are banned forever as vocabulary. They are blocked because the current GRI evidence does not operationally support them. A stronger term can return only when a native definition, estimator, uncertainty model, prospective falsifier, and appropriate independent evidence jointly earn it.

Narrative convenience, visual symmetry, resemblance to the physical damping formula, or proximity of a value to unity are not reinstatement criteria.
