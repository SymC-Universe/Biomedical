## LC-09 — Dimensional / transdiagnostic psychopathology and comorbidity

Disposition: `ANSWERED_AS_A_GENERAL_RESEARCH DIRECTION / OPEN_FOR_NSD-SPECIFIC PHYSIOLOGICAL ARCHITECTURE`.

### Established prior art

A dimensional or transdiagnostic view of psychopathology is already a major research lineage.

- Caspi et al. (2014), *The p Factor: One General Psychopathology Factor in the Structure of Psychiatric Disorders?*, examined dimensionality, persistence, co-occurrence, and sequential comorbidity across two decades and proposed a general psychopathology factor.
- NIMH Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) explicitly organizes mental-health research around dimensions of neurobehavioral functioning and transdiagnostic dysfunction rather than treating categorical diagnoses as the only scientific units.
- HiTOP formalizes a hierarchical dimensional taxonomy based on symptom covariance and explicitly addresses heterogeneity, comorbidity, and arbitrary categorical boundaries.
- Large HiTOP meta-analytic and neurobiological literatures now exist.

### Consequence for the historical NSD claim

The 2025 formulation that psychiatric categories are misleading because disorders overlap, vary continuously, or share underlying structure is **not a novel scientific contribution by itself**.

The historical phrase that the “comorbidity paradox dissolves” also exceeds what can be claimed from the available evidence. Existing dimensional frameworks already supply non-categorical explanations of comorbidity without requiring a common damping coordinate.

### Residual NSD question

The remaining question is more specific:

> Does a frozen neurophysiological Stability Architecture identify reproducible shared and disorder-specific organization beyond established symptom-dimensional, transdiagnostic, and conventional neurophysiological representations?

A successful NSD result would complement rather than replace RDoC/HiTOP/p-factor approaches unless empirical evidence eventually supports a stronger distinction.

## LC-10 — Normative modeling and independent healthy-reference architecture

Disposition: `ESTABLISHED METHOD FAMILY / OPEN_FOR_NSD-SPECIFIC INTEGRATION`.

### Established prior art

Normative modeling is already well developed in computational psychiatry and clinical neuroscience.

- Rutherford et al. (2022), *The normative modeling framework for computational psychiatry*, Nature Protocols 17:1711-1734, provides a standardized framework for individual deviations relative to population reference models.
- Resting-state EEG connectivity normative deviations have been used to predict antidepressant response using healthy-reference data from multiple cohorts.
- Hassan et al. (2025), *Beyond homogeneity: charting the landscape of heterogeneity in neurodevelopmental and psychiatric electroencephalography*, Translational Psychiatry 15:223, applied HD-EEG normative modeling to 1,674 patients with ADHD, autism, learning disorder, or anxiety and 560 matched controls. Spectral-power and connectivity deviations were highly heterogeneous and incompletely spatially overlapping among patients.

### Consequence for NSD

An independent healthy-reference Atlas is scientifically sensible, but “compare each patient to a healthy norm” is not novel.

The Atlas must therefore be justified by its role in the NSD architecture rather than by a claim of inventing normative modeling.

### Residual NSD question

Potential residual contribution lies in combining a normative reference with:
- an independently qualified label-blind Structural Engine;
- model-specific dynamical-scalar admission and refusal;
- spectral, modal, spatial, and conglomerate layers;
- explicit absent-feature and open-channel phenotypes;
- stable Engine/Atlas version separation;
- Function and Limit Maps;
- direct comparison with conventional normative spectral/connectivity models.

Whether this combination is novel remains `CANDIDATE` until targeted prior-art searching closes each component and the integrated package.

## LC-11 — Patient heterogeneity is already a central EEG problem

Disposition: `ANSWERED / NOT NOVEL`.

Large EEG normative-modeling work now directly demonstrates that patients within the same diagnostic category can show highly heterogeneous individual deviations and limited spatial overlap.

This matters because the old NSD architecture visually assigned diagnostic groups to relatively clean attractor regions. The forward program must expect and preserve within-diagnosis heterogeneity rather than treating it as noise around a disorder centroid.

Residual NSD question:

> Can the multilevel architecture reveal reproducible substructure or cross-disorder relationships without erasing the individual heterogeneity already established by normative EEG work?

## Updated collision boundary

The following broad ideas are now explicitly outside NSD's novelty claim:

- dimensional psychiatry;
- transdiagnostic psychopathology;
- comorbidity as evidence of shared latent structure;
- normative healthy-reference modeling;
- individualized deviation maps;
- psychiatric EEG heterogeneity;
- periodic/aperiodic EEG decomposition;
- neural damped-oscillator/Q/pole modeling;
- generic EEG classification or outcome prediction.

The project should therefore be judged on the narrower residual program defined in `NOVELTY_ATTRIBUTION_AUDIT_v0.1.md`, not on the breadth of the 2025 conceptual claims.
