## A. Peak absence and categorical oscillatory morphology

### Prior art found

Peak absence is already explicitly represented in established neural-spectral practice.

- SpecParam documentation states that when no peak is detected there is no evidence, within the fitted spectrum, for periodic activity above the aperiodic component. It also warns that absence of a detected peak does not prove absence of an oscillation because weak or bursty activity may fail to produce a prominent peak.
- Automated alpha-characterization work has long classified recordings into no distinguishable alpha peak, one alpha peak, and two alpha peaks while retaining spatial information.
- A 2024 longitudinal portable-EEG study reported stable individual differences including no alpha peak, one alpha peak, or two alpha peaks over repeated observations.
- Clinical and intracranial studies sometimes exclude subjects with no qualifying peak when the scientific question requires a peak, making absence an explicit analytical state rather than a hidden zero.

### Consequence

The broad idea “absence of a peak can be represented categorically” is **not novel**.

The candidate residual is narrower:

> Does a unified NSD Engine preserve and differentiate multiple non-admission states, such as true no-peak morphology, insufficient signal, poor fit, unresolved multi-mode structure, dynamical non-identifiability, broadening ambiguity, and cross-model disagreement, and do any of those states carry reproducible information after technical causes are controlled?

Status: `CANDIDATE SYNTHETIC CONTRIBUTION`, not component novelty.

## B. Model non-identifiability is established science

### Prior art found

Parameter identifiability is a standard concern in state-space, neural-population, and dynamical modeling.

- EEG state-space models explicitly discuss identifiability constraints.
- Neural-population model studies have shown that many physiological parameters may be poorly identifiable from EEG spectra even when certain parameter combinations are identifiable.
- More general system-identification practice treats non-identifiability as a formal property to diagnose rather than as a value to impute.

### Consequence

NSD cannot claim novelty for recognizing that a neural dynamical model can be non-identifiable or for refusing an unidentifiable parameter in principle.

Candidate residual:

> operationalizing identifiability, signal sufficiency, model agreement, and scalar-admission status as first-class outputs inside one clinical-neurophysiology architecture, then testing their reliability and incremental information.

Again, this is likely a synthesis/governance contribution unless empirical results reveal a new phenotype.

## C. Integrated spatiotemporal modal analysis is established

### Prior art found

The modal/vector layer also has strong external lineage.

- Brunton et al. (2016), *Extracting spatial–temporal coherent patterns in large-scale neural recordings using dynamic mode decomposition*, adapted Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) to neural recordings and extracted coupled spatial-temporal modes, including sleep spindle networks with characteristic cortical distribution, frequency, and duration.
- EEG/brain system-identification literature has compared Output-Only Modal Analysis and DMD, yielding eigenmode decompositions of spatiotemporal brain dynamics.
- State-space oscillator models explicitly distinguish oscillatory components from broadband noise using complex poles and model selection.
- DMD methods produce complex eigenvalues with frequency plus growth/decay and spatial participation patterns.
- DMD has been used in clinical/neuropsychiatric settings, including MDD fMRI, EEG/ECoG decoding, and recent EEG disorder-detection work.

### Consequence

NSD cannot claim novelty for:
- combining space and time into modes;
- extracting oscillatory frequencies plus growth/decay rates;
- using complex eigenvalues/poles;
- preserving spatial mode shapes;
- using modal features for clinical classification.

This is a major and productive collision because it identifies mature native methods that should become **mandatory comparators and candidate Engine implementations**, not competitors to be ignored.

## E. New highest-value scientific opportunity

The deepest residual opportunity is becoming less about inventing a new analysis primitive and more about a **testable scientific conjunction**:

1. start with accepted spectral and modal/system-identification methods;
2. explicitly qualify which dynamical parameters are identifiable in the target neural data;
3. refuse unsupported scalar compression;
4. preserve ordinary spectral, spatial, modal, and missing/ambiguous structure;
5. use a fixed normative/reference layer;
6. test whether a stability-oriented organization of those already legitimate quantities reveals reproducible shared-versus-specific clinical structure that stronger existing methods do not already explain.

If that conjunction produces no incremental structure, NSD should narrow accordingly. If it does, the empirical result, not the existence of DMD, SpecParam, normative modeling, or dimensional psychiatry, is where the scientific contribution lies.

## F. Implementation consequence

The Engine specification should treat the following as serious native candidates rather than inventing replacements unnecessarily:
- periodic/aperiodic parameterization for spectral state;
- state-space oscillator models for explicit oscillatory components;
- Output-Only Modal Analysis and/or DMD for spatiotemporal modes;
- neural-mass/generative models where biological interpretability is required and identifiability can be defended;
- normative modeling for subject-level reference deviation.

The research question then becomes whether the Stability Architecture **organizes and constrains** these components in a scientifically useful way.

## G. Novelty language ceiling tightened

Until further evidence, do not call the architecture itself “novel” merely because these components are assembled under NSD.

Safer wording:
- “we integrate”;
- “we impose a stability-oriented qualification architecture”;
- “we test whether”;
- “we compare against”;
- “we preserve refusal/non-identifiability explicitly.”

The strongest eventual novelty claim, if earned, should be tied to a specific empirical finding or validated methodological capability that survives these native comparators.
