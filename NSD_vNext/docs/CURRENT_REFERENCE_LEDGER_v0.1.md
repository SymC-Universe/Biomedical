# NSD Current Reference Ledger v0.1

Date: 14 September 2026
Status: ACTIVE / CLAIM-LEVEL SOURCE REBUILD
Purpose: replace the historical bibliography-without-inline-citations problem with an explicit claim-to-source record.

This ledger is not a complete manuscript bibliography. It records what each source is currently allowed to support and, equally important, what it does **not** establish.

## A. Dimensional and transdiagnostic psychopathology

### A1. Caspi A, Houts RM, Belsky DW, et al. (2014)
*The p Factor: One General Psychopathology Factor in the Structure of Psychiatric Disorders?* Clinical Psychological Science 2(2):119-137. DOI `10.1177/2167702613497473`.

Supports:
- psychiatric disorders can show dimensionality, persistence, co-occurrence, and sequential comorbidity;
- a general psychopathology factor is one established transdiagnostic model;
- transdiagnostic framing predates NSD.

Does not support:
- a neural damping ratio as the p factor;
- a universal stability coordinate;
- NSD's specific physiological architecture.

### A2. NIMH Research Domain Criteria (RDoC)
NIMH research framework.

Supports:
- studying psychopathology through dimensions of neurobehavioral functioning;
- transdiagnostic research that is not organized solely around DSM categories;
- deviations from normal-function systems as a legitimate research strategy.

Does not support:
- a particular NSD scalar or modal implementation;
- elimination of clinical diagnoses.

### A3. Kotov R, Krueger RF, Watson D, et al. (2021)
*The Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology (HiTOP): A Quantitative Nosology Based on Consensus of Evidence.* Annual Review of Clinical Psychology 17:83-108. DOI `10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-081219-093304`.

Supports:
- dimensional/hierarchical psychopathology;
- heterogeneity and comorbidity as major nosological problems;
- symptom-covariance based transdiagnostic structure.

Does not support:
- a common neural dynamical mechanism;
- equivalence between symptom hierarchy and Stability Architecture.

## B. Normative modeling and individual deviation

### B1. Rutherford S, Kia SM, Wolfers T, et al. (2022)
*The normative modeling framework for computational psychiatry.* Nature Protocols 17:1711-1734. DOI `10.1038/s41596-022-00696-5`.

Supports:
- individual deviation relative to population/reference models;
- normative modeling as established computational-psychiatry infrastructure;
- moving beyond group-average case-control designs.

Does not support:
- NSD Atlas novelty by itself;
- any specific stability quantity.

### B2. *Individual deviations from normative electroencephalographic connectivity predict antidepressant response* (2024)
Journal of Affective Disorders 351:220-230. DOI `10.1016/j.jad.2024.01.177`.

Supports:
- healthy-reference normative modeling can be applied to resting EEG connectivity;
- individual EEG deviations can support treatment-response prediction;
- EEG normative modeling predates the forward NSD Atlas.

Does not support:
- stability-dynamical interpretation of those deviations;
- general psychiatric prediction from NSD.

### B3. Hassan M, et al. (2025)
*Beyond homogeneity: charting the landscape of heterogeneity in neurodevelopmental and psychiatric electroencephalography.* Translational Psychiatry 15:223.

Supports:
- HD-EEG normative modeling in large neurodevelopmental/psychiatric cohorts;
- strong within-diagnosis heterogeneity;
- spectral-power and connectivity deviations with incomplete spatial overlap across patients;
- the need to preserve individual heterogeneity rather than only group centroids.

Does not support:
- one shared stability class;
- clean diagnosis-specific attractor basins.

## C. Periodic / aperiodic spectral modeling

### C1. Donoghue T, Haller M, Peterson EJ, et al. (2020)
*Parameterizing neural power spectra into periodic and aperiodic components.* Nature Neuroscience 23:1655-1665. DOI `10.1038/s41593-020-00744-x`.

Supports:
- separate periodic peaks from aperiodic background;
- narrowband power can be misleading when no true peak is present;
- descriptive peak center frequency, power, and bandwidth;
- explicit no-peak states.

Does not support:
- Gaussian bandwidth as a damping rate;
- aperiodic exponent as direct chi;
- every spectral peak as a unique neural oscillator.

### C2. McKeown MJ, et al. (2023/2024)
*Test-retest reliability of spectral parameterization by 1/f characterization using SpecParam.* Cerebral Cortex 34(1):bhad482. DOI `10.1093/cercor/bhad482`.

Supports:
- some periodic and aperiodic parameters show useful test-retest reliability;
- reliability varies by state, feature, and fit condition;
- simple spectral reliability is not an unanswered NSD novelty question.

Does not support:
- reliability of NSD's full architecture;
- reliability of a dynamical chi.

### C3. Salvatore S, et al. (2024)
*Periodic and aperiodic changes to cortical EEG in response to pharmacological manipulation.* Journal of Neurophysiology 131:529-540. DOI `10.1152/jn.00445.2023`.

Supports:
- aperiodic exponent responds to neurophysiological manipulation;
- it is not a universally reliable marker of cortical excitation/inhibition ratio.

Does not support:
- direct exponent -> gamma -> chi conversion.

## D. Psychiatric / neurodevelopmental EEG heterogeneity

### D1. Newson JJ, Thiagarajan TC (2019)
*EEG Frequency Bands in Psychiatric Disorders: A Review of Resting State Studies.* Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 12:521. DOI `10.3389/fnhum.2018.00521`.

Supports:
- substantial overlap and inconsistency of conventional resting EEG findings across psychiatric diagnoses;
- generic transdiagnostic EEG overlap is not novel.

Does not support:
- a common physical mechanism;
- NSD's specific architecture.

### D2. Neo WS, et al. (2023)
*Resting-state EEG power differences in autism spectrum disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* Translational Psychiatry.

Supports:
- ASD resting EEG case-control differences have already been meta-analyzed;
- substantial heterogeneity exists across studies/features;
- basic ASD band-power separation is not the forward novelty target.

Does not support:
- mode-specific stability differences;
- cross-disorder NSD structure.

## E. Explicit neural oscillator / modal dynamics

### E1. Hindriks R, van Putten MJAM, Deco G (2011)
*Dynamics underlying spontaneous human alpha oscillations: A data-driven approach.* NeuroImage 57:440-451. DOI `10.1016/j.neuroimage.2011.04.043`.

Supports:
- human MEG alpha can be modeled, in a specific setting, with second-order stochastic damped-oscillator dynamics;
- neural damping is not inherently metaphorical.

Does not support:
- applying the same model to every scalp EEG peak;
- universal clinical damping thresholds.

### E2. Spyropoulos G, et al. (2022)
*Spontaneous variability in gamma dynamics described by a damped harmonic oscillator driven by noise.* Nature Communications 13:2019. DOI `10.1038/s41467-022-29674-x`.

Supports:
- a noise-driven damped harmonic oscillator can reproduce specific macaque V1 gamma dynamics;
- an explicit dynamical model can connect oscillation properties to an E-I circuit in that setting.

Does not support:
- direct generalization to resting psychiatric scalp EEG.

### E3. Moran RJ, Kiebel SJ, Stephan KE, Reilly RB, Daunizeau J, Friston KJ (2007)
*A neural mass model of spectral responses in electrophysiology.* NeuroImage 37:706-720. DOI `10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.05.032`.

Supports:
- generative neural-mass spectral modeling;
- complex-pole / transfer-function descriptions of neural dynamics;
- model-based routes to frequency and decay/growth structure.

Does not support:
- parameter identifiability in arbitrary datasets;
- NSD scalar admission without separate qualification.

### E4. Beck AM, Stephen EP, Purdon PL (2018)
*State Space Oscillator Models for Neural Data Analysis.* IEEE EMBC 2018:4740-4743. DOI `10.1109/EMBC.2018.8513215`.

Supports:
- state-space models with explicit oscillator structure can separate oscillatory components from broadband noise;
- model selection can be used to infer oscillatory composition.

Does not support:
- universal oscillator presence;
- whole-brain scalar reduction.

### E5. Brunton BW, Johnson LA, Ojemann JG, Kutz JN (2016)
*Extracting spatial-temporal coherent patterns in large-scale neural recordings using dynamic mode decomposition.* Journal of Neuroscience Methods 258:1-15. DOI `10.1016/j.jneumeth.2015.10.010`.

Supports:
- DMD can extract coupled spatiotemporal neural modes;
- spatial participation, frequency, and temporal dynamics can be integrated in one modal representation;
- modal + spatial architecture is not novel by itself.

Does not support:
- a psychiatric stability interpretation without further evidence.

### E6. *System identification methods for dynamic models of brain activity* (2021)
Biomedical Signal Processing and Control 68:102765. DOI `10.1016/j.bspc.2021.102765`.

Supports:
- Output-Only Modal Analysis and Dynamic Mode Decomposition are established candidate system-identification methods for EEG/brain dynamics;
- eigenmode decompositions can represent spatiotemporal neural dynamics.

Does not support:
- superiority of a specific NSD implementation without direct comparison.

## F. Identifiability and refusal

### F1. Hartoyo A, Cadusch PJ, Liley DTJ, Hicks DG (2019)
*Parameter estimation and identifiability in a neural population model for electro-cortical activity.* PLoS Computational Biology 15(5):e1006694. DOI `10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006694`.

Supports:
- many neural-population model parameters can be poorly identifiable from EEG spectra;
- some parameter combinations may remain identifiable even when individual parameters are not;
- parameter identifiability must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

Does not support:
- treating every non-identifiable fit as a biological phenotype.

### F2. SpecParam documentation / Donoghue et al. framework

Supports:
- no detected peak is a legitimate analytical state;
- absence of a detected peak is not proof of physiological absence;
- burstiness/weak oscillations can evade spectral-peak detection.

Does not support:
- interpreting all peak absence as disease.

### F3. Multiple-alpha-peak / no-peak literature

Established studies have explicitly categorized spectra into no alpha peak, one alpha peak, or multiple alpha peaks.

Supports:
- categorical spectral morphology is established prior art;
- NSD's possible contribution must be broader than merely retaining no-peak states.

## G. Q factor / linewidth precedent

### G1. Jin Y, et al. (2012)
*Alpha EEG guided TMS in schizophrenia.* Brain Stimulation 5:560-568. DOI `10.1016/j.brs.2011.09.005`.

Supports:
- clinical EEG literature has previously used alpha peak frequency and half-power bandwidth to form a Q-like measure;
- Q-factor language in EEG is not novel.

Does not support:
- universal conversion from any descriptive peak width into mechanical damping ratio;
- robust clinical utility without stronger validation.

## H. Source-use rule

For every manuscript statement that survives into a submission draft, add:
- claim ID;
- source ID(s) from this ledger or later extension;
- exact supported wording;
- maturity class;
- what the source cannot establish.

This prevents the old failure mode where a bibliography existed but the reader could not tell which reference supported which exact assertion.
