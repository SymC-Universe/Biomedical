# NSD Known Spectra vs Stability-Architecture Mapping v0.1

**Status:** LITERATURE-MAPPING / DEVELOPMENT ONLY  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Governance:** SymC GOM v1.1 + Continuity Hardening Addendum  
**Scope:** map already-established clinical, behavioral, neurophysiological, network, developmental, and transdiagnostic dimensions against candidate NSD stability variables.  
**Scientific ceiling:** no claim that \(\chi\), \(\Chi\), or \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) is clinically valid, diagnostically useful, or superior to an existing spectrum.

## 1. Root question

OG NSD sought a real neural-stability map rather than a diagnosis-only map. The current task is therefore not to invent another "spectrum" in parallel with autism, ADHD, psychosis, dementia, pain, or other established dimensions.

The task is:

> For each established spectrum or dimensional framework already used in neuroscience and psychiatry, determine whether a qualified NSD stability variable is redundant with it, conditionally related to it, orthogonal and incrementally informative, subgroup-specific, state-specific, or refused.

The comparison unit is always the native construct first. Stability variables are admitted only afterward.

## 2. The spectrum families that must be mapped

### A. Clinical / phenotypic dimensions

Examples:
- autism symptom and trait dimensions, including clinical ASD and broader autistic traits;
- social-communication difficulty;
- restricted/repetitive behavior;
- sensory hyper/hyporeactivity;
- adaptive functioning;
- attention, impulsivity, hyperactivity;
- internalizing, externalizing, psychosis-spectrum, compulsivity, somatic and related symptom dimensions;
- cognitive and functional impairment;
- severity, progression, and treatment-response dimensions.

Primary question:
- does a stability variable covary with a phenotypic dimension, distinguish biologically different people with similar phenotypes, or add no information?

### B. Transdiagnostic dimensional frameworks

#### RDoC
RDoC organizes functional constructs across the continuum from normal to abnormal and measures them across genes, molecules, cells, circuits, physiology, behavior, self-report, and paradigms.

Current domains include:
- Negative Valence Systems;
- Positive Valence Systems;
- Cognitive Systems;
- Systems for Social Processes;
- Sensorimotor Systems;
- Arousal/Regulatory Systems.

NSD must not relabel an RDoC construct as stability. Instead, test whether stability coordinates predict or organize variation within a declared RDoC construct.

#### HiTOP
HiTOP is a hierarchical dimensional taxonomy spanning super-spectra, spectra, subfactors, symptom components, maladaptive traits, symptoms, and maladaptive behaviors.

NSD comparison must be level-matched:
- local neural stability cannot be compared directly with a whole HiTOP super-spectrum without an explicit bridge model;
- any association must state whether the target is symptom-level, trait-level, spectrum-level, or higher-order psychopathology.

### C. Classical EEG spectral dimensions

Native quantities include:
- delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma power;
- absolute and relative power;
- peak/center frequency;
- cross-frequency relationships;
- event-related spectral perturbations.

Autism literature already reports group differences and substantial heterogeneity in these measures. Therefore a new NSD coordinate is not novel merely because it changes across ASD or across symptom severity.

Key comparison:
- does \(\chi\) explain information beyond ordinary frequency/power structure after age, task, reference, and recording conditions are controlled?

### D. Aperiodic / scale-free EEG dimensions

Candidate native quantities:
- aperiodic exponent/slope;
- offset;
- knee where applicable;
- separation of oscillatory peaks from aperiodic background.

These measures are sometimes interpreted in relation to excitation/inhibition or population timescales, but that mapping is not universally direct.

NSD test:
- determine whether a qualified local \(\chi\) is merely a reparameterization/correlate of aperiodic structure or provides distinct dynamical information.

### E. Excitation / inhibition continua

E/I balance is an established mechanistic framework in autism and other neuropsychiatric disorders, but the literature explicitly warns that different measurement approaches produce different findings and that ASD does not support a simple universal "too much excitation" rule.

Native evidence can include:
- glutamate/GABA MRS;
- TMS measures;
- pharmacologic challenge;
- cellular/synaptic measures;
- computational circuit inference;
- electrophysiological proxies.

NSD rule:
- \(\chi\) cannot be called an E/I measure unless a model-specific mapping is derived and validated.
- test whether \(\chi\) varies independently of, downstream from, or as a function of E/I measures.

### F. Connectivity and network-organization dimensions

Native measures include:
- functional/effective connectivity;
- coherence/phase-locking;
- graph topology;
- network integration/segregation;
- modularity;
- hubness;
- dynamic functional connectivity;
- EEG microstate topology, duration, occurrence, coverage, transition probabilities;
- network hierarchy and state transitions.

A 2026 EEG microstate study already reports graded neural-dynamics organization across low autistic traits, high autistic traits, and diagnosed ASD. This is a direct prior-art collision for any claim that "graded neural dynamics across the autism spectrum" is itself novel.

NSD test:
- \(\Chi\) must demonstrate a precise relation to native modal/network organization and incremental information beyond the native network measures.
- \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) cannot be inferred merely because a network metric is abnormal.

### G. Neural variability / noise dimensions

Native quantities include:
- trial-to-trial variability;
- within-subject spectral variability;
- response variability;
- entropy/complexity measures;
- state-transition variability.

Autism literature has long proposed increased or altered intra-individual neural variability as a meaningful dimension.

NSD test:
- determine whether instability/refusal in \(\chi\) tracks variability itself or whether variability and stability are separable properties.

### H. Criticality / metastability / dynamical-regime dimensions

Candidate native constructs include:
- distance to bifurcation/critical transition;
- neuronal avalanche statistics;
- critical slowing;
- metastability;
- synchrony/desynchrony;
- multistability;
- Lyapunov or related local stability descriptors where identifiable;
- recovery from perturbation.

These are the closest conceptual neighbors to NSD.

NSD novelty burden is therefore high:
- \(\chi\) must not be presented as a generic "distance from criticality" without an explicit mapping;
- \(\Chi\) and \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) must be compared against native state-space, modal, criticality, and recovery descriptions.

### I. Developmental and normative spectra

Native approaches include:
- age-related developmental trajectories;
- centiles/normative modeling;
- individual deviation scores;
- sex, IQ, developmental stage, medication, sleep, and environment effects;
- longitudinal within-person change.

Normative modeling is increasingly used because group averages hide individual heterogeneity.

NSD test:
- a subject's stability coordinate should be interpreted against a developmentally appropriate normative distribution rather than against a single pooled "healthy" threshold.

### J. State / trait / context spectra

Potential axes:
- wake vs sleep;
- arousal;
- medication state;
- task vs rest;
- sensory load;
- stress/threat;
- fatigue;
- circadian phase;
- acute vs chronic pain;
- seizure/interictal state;
- intervention/pre-post state.

NSD must distinguish:
- trait-like stability architecture;
- temporary state displacement;
- measurement/context artifact;
- true longitudinal reorganization.

## 3. Stability-architecture levels

### Local scalar \(\chi\)

Question:
- does a licensed local dynamical mode admit a scalar damping/stability coordinate?

It may relate to:
- oscillation frequency;
- decay/recovery;
- E/I;
- aperiodic timescale;
- spectral peak shape;
- local perturbation response.

None of these relations is automatic.

### Modal/vector \(\Chi\)

Question:
- what multidimensional dynamical organization must be retained after scalarization is insufficient or refused?

Potential contents:
- modal frequencies/damping;
- eigenspaces/subspaces;
- participation;
- coupling;
- non-normality;
- transient amplification;
- observation dependence;
- uncertainty.

### System architecture \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\)

Question:
- what qualified organization exists across local modes, network/coupling structure, state transitions, recovery, hierarchy, history, and phenotype?

It is not a scalar and is not automatically licensed by either \(\chi\) or \(\Chi\).

## 4. Relationship classes

For every existing spectrum S and every candidate stability object X, classify the relationship as one of:

1. **REDUNDANT**  
   X is approximately a reparameterization or highly predictable transformation of S.

2. **CONDITIONAL / MEDIATED**  
   S and X are related only under a specified model, state, subgroup, or mechanism.

3. **ORTHOGONAL / INCREMENTAL**  
   X explains reproducible held-out information after native S is included.

4. **SUBGROUP-SPECIFIC**  
   Relationship exists only in a biologically or clinically defensible subgroup.

5. **STATE-SPECIFIC**  
   Relationship changes with task, sleep, arousal, medication, perturbation, or context.

6. **NONIDENTIFIABLE / NEED_MORE_INFO**  
   Available data cannot distinguish the relationship.

7. **REFUSED**  
   The proposed stability object is not licensed for that dataset/model.

These are descriptive relationship classes, not rankings.

## 5. Required comparison tests

For each mapped spectrum:

- native-model baseline first;
- age/development/context adjustment;
- within-person vs between-person decomposition;
- linear and nonlinear dependence tests;
- uncertainty-aware correlation/association;
- held-out incremental prediction;
- nested model comparison;
- subgroup and interaction tests;
- site/reference/task sensitivity;
- test-retest reliability;
- longitudinal or perturbation-recovery validation where data exist;
- explicit refusal when the stability object is not identifiable.

A stability coordinate only "adds" if it improves a declared held-out target beyond the strongest native comparator without post-result tuning.

### Then the NSD collision map
For each literature family, record:
- construct;
- measurement;
- sample/population;
- state/task;
- known directionality;
- reproducibility/heterogeneity;
- mechanism claimed;
- limitations;
- nearest \(\chi\) object;

- nearest \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) object;
- redundancy risk;
- discriminating experiment.

## 9. Working hypothesis

The strongest current NSD hypothesis is not that there is one neural-stability spectrum replacing existing clinical spectra.

It is:

> Established clinical, behavioral, developmental, and neurophysiological spectra describe different projections of neural organization. A qualified stability architecture may provide an additional dynamical coordinate system that explains how those projections are generated, related, or reorganized, but only where it demonstrates held-out incremental information beyond native dimensional measures.

Failure is informative. If \(\chi\) is redundant with an established native measure, record that. If \(\Chi\) adds nothing beyond native network dynamics, record that. If \(\Chi_{\mathrm{arc}}\) cannot be uniquely constructed, refuse it.
