## Compatibility map

The prior-art components are compatible with a local damping-like scalar only under a restricted conjunction:

1. a native biological model or empirically justified local linearization supplies a dynamical mode;
2. the relevant local realization is identifiable/observable;
3. the mode is genuinely second order or has a licensed equivalent complex-pair reduction;
4. continuous-time reconstruction is alias-safe or otherwise uniquely constrained;
5. the observed/subnetwork coordinates are sufficiently closed for the local claim, or unresolved memory is explicitly modeled;
6. stochastic forcing does not create an oscillatory appearance that is being mistaken for complex deterministic poles;
7. uncertainty is propagated through the pole-to-(chi) mapping;
8. the local coordinate is not used as a substitute for broader modal/vector (Chi) or realized perturbation/recovery behavior.

These conditions come from established pieces of prior art. Their conjunction is the inherited scientific baseline, not an empirical Bio Chi result.

## Integrated prior-art picture

The literature already establishes that biological oscillators can be analyzed through feedback models, pole/eigenvalue geometry, damping/coherence measures, and system identification. It also establishes that identifiability, sampling, stochastic forcing, projection, hidden-state memory, and model reduction can change or invalidate the apparent low-order dynamics.

Therefore the broad claim "biology has a damping ratio" is neither novel nor generally licensed. Likewise, the observation of an oscillatory spectrum is insufficient to establish a second-order biological mode.

What remains scientifically useful is an explicit operational framework that makes the prior-art conditions act as an **admission/refusal architecture** before a local scalar is reported, while keeping local (chi), modal/vector (Chi), and system-level perturbation/recovery evidence distinct.

## Conglomerated implications

1. A biological local (chi) is best treated as a **conditional coordinate of a qualified dynamical lineage**, not as a generic summary of oscillation.
2. Failure of local-scalar admission does not imply absence of biological stability structure; it can instead redirect interpretation to modal/vector or system-level architecture.
3. Same local pole geometry does not by itself establish equivalent embedded recovery behavior because projection, coupling, non-normality, history, and hidden-state memory can remain consequential.
4. Sampling invariance and projection closure are not cosmetic diagnostics. They are part of determining whether the same continuous dynamical object has been identified.

These are currently P0-N synthesis conclusions. Realized biological validity requires new qualification/empirical evidence.

## Contradictions and gaps

- Biological systems often move among damped, sustained, adaptive, refractory, multistable, and stochastic regimes; one fixed scalar family is not universally appropriate.
- Low-order reduction may depend on sampling window, projection, and unobserved states.
- Existing methods address individual gates, but the search did not identify a single prior workflow that prospectively integrates native-model licensing, model order, identifiability, continuous-time sampling, closure/memory, uncertainty, refusal, and separation of local scalar from broader system stability.
- The prevalence of regimes that pass such a gate in real biology is unknown.
- A practically reliable finite-data structural-order refusal remains unresolved in the current Bio Chi qualification work.

The residual is **not** the damping ratio, Q factor, pole geometry, biological oscillation analysis, identifiability analysis, aliasing theory, or Mori-Zwanzig memory. Those are established.

The candidate contribution is the prospectively testable integration of these established safeguards into a biological local-(chi) admission/refusal workflow and Function/Limit map, plus explicit separation and joint interpretation of local (chi), broader (Chi), and realized system behavior.

## Residual claim

A biological local (chi) should be reported only when a native or scientifically licensed reduced dynamical representation supports an identifiable continuous-time mode and passes prospectively defined sampling, closure/memory, uncertainty, and refusal checks. The current project seeks to determine whether such a workflow can recover the correct local coordinate across representative valid regimes while refusing or downgrading incompatible regimes without erasing broader (Chi)/system information.

This is an exploratory methodological target, not a confirmed biological law.

## Why new evidence is needed

Prior work establishes the individual ingredients and important counterexamples, but does not establish how reliably the integrated gate behaves across representative valid modes, boundary regimes, and biological data under one fixed prospective implementation.

New evidence is needed to determine:

- coverage of the supported interior;
- false-refusal rate inside the intended family;
- false-admission behavior outside it;
- sensitivity to sampling, memory, forcing geometry, and finite-sample uncertainty;
- and whether any real biological testbed can satisfy the admission conditions without manufacturing the scalar from descriptive summaries.
