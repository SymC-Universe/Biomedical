## Question

What information about perturbation, transient amplification, and recovery is preserved or lost by progressively richer neural stability representations?

The candidate representation ladder is:

1. local modal damping coordinates only;
2. local modal vector plus embedded eigenspectrum;
3. eigenspectrum plus non-normal/reactivity information;
4. transient propagator/gain structure;
5. recovery/return structure under declared perturbations;
6. broader modal/vector and system/conglomerate representation where the above remain identifiable.

No single whole-system chi is assumed.

## Existing internal known truth

The completed coupled-second-order fixture already establishes that:

- identical local damping ratios plus identical full eigenspectra can produce different transient gain and recovery;
- identical local damping ratios can coexist with different embedded modal organization;
- locally stable modes can coexist with a globally unstable coupled system.

Canonical source:
`NSD_vNext/docs/CHI_SYSTEM_COUPLED_KNOWN_TRUTH_POSTRESULT_v0.1.md`.

This is sufficient to reject local-chi-only and eigenspectrum-only sufficiency in those exact fixtures, but it is not yet a broad representation-sufficiency map.

## Prior-art collision

Prior work on non-normal neural dynamics already establishes that eigenspectra describe asymptotic stability/timescale information but are insufficient for transient behavior, and that eigenvector alignment/non-normal feed-forward structure can control transient amplification. The neural-network literature therefore owns the generic claim that spectrum alone is insufficient for transient dynamics.

Relevant collision points include:

- transient amplification from non-normal structure in abstract and biological neural networks;
- network organization shaping collective neural function;
- long-standing neural-network work on sustained, oscillatory, chaotic, and stimulus-driven dynamics.

Therefore the residual NSD question cannot be “does non-normality matter?” or “are eigenvalues insufficient?” Those are prior art.

## Residual candidate contribution

The candidate residual is a governed **representation-sufficiency Function/Limit map** that asks, prospectively and quantitatively:

- which representation layers preserve the declared perturbation/recovery observables;
- where lower-dimensional representations become equivalent to richer ones;
- where richer representations add genuine predictive or reconstructive information;
- where representation itself becomes nonidentifiable or sampling dependent;
- how local chi and broader Chi/system structure should be interpreted together rather than substituted for one another.

This is currently classified as `NEW_INTEGRATION` / candidate `NEW_DISCRIMINATING_TEST`, not a novel principle claim.
