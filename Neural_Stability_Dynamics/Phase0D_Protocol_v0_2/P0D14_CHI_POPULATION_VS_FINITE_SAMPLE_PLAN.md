# P0-D14 Population vs Finite-Sample Chi Branch Diagnosis Plan

Date: 2026-09-11
Status: **P0-D ESTIMATOR / IDENTIFIABILITY DIAGNOSIS. NOT ATLAS CALIBRATION. NOT P0-Q. NOT P1.**

## Question

P0-D13 preserved target-lineage identity but finite-sample SSI-COV often reconstructed a stable complex pair after the known truth had crossed to two stable real poles. Does that branch compression already exist in the exact population covariance-Hankel realization, or is it generated by finite-record estimation noise and weak support of the second direction?

## Population construction

For the known continuous generator `A`, sampling interval `dt`, process covariance `Q`, and output map `C`:

1. compute `F = expm(A dt)`;
2. solve the discrete stationary Lyapunov equation `P = F P F^T + Q`;
3. compute exact positive-lag output covariances
   `R_l = C F^l P C^T`;
4. assemble the exact same block covariance Hankel geometry used by current NSD SSI-COV:
   `H_ij = R_(i+j+1)`;
5. run the current fixed-order SSI realization on this population Hankel.

If population recovery is accurate, the estimator architecture can represent the branch in principle and the observed P0-D11/P0-D13 compression is finite-sample/conditioning related.

## Surface

For each chi:

### P: exact population Hankel

Record exact recovered poles, chi, branch, `s2/s1`, and the numerical population tail `s3/s1`.

### F1/F2: finite samples

Simulate 8 independent records at:

- `n=8000`;
- `n=32000`.

For each clean record, evaluate both:

- clean output;
- output plus 3% channel-SD independent white measurement noise.

The same underlying process record is reused for clean/noisy comparison. No Atlas information is used.

## Diagnostics

No pass threshold is defined. Track:

- successful pair-invariant chi reconstruction;
- median chi estimate and absolute error;
- complex-vs-real fitted pair fraction;
- `s2/s1` structural support;
- finite-sample `s2/s3` gap;
- pole stability;
- theoretical fast/slow pole magnitude ratio on the overdamped branch.

## Important expectation

Independent white measurement noise does not alter the **population positive-lag cross-covariance** for nonzero lag, but it can increase finite-sample covariance variance. Therefore a clean/noisy difference here is an uncertainty/resolution effect rather than a population positive-lag bias.
