# Neurostability Atlas Coordinate Map v0.1

Date: 2026-09-11
Protocol: General Cross-Project Research Protocol v0.7.1 FINAL + authoritative v0.7.1A Addendum
System Model basis: NSD System Model v1.0, architecture locked
Status: **ATLAS P0-D COORDINATE ONTOLOGY / SOURCE-MAPPING STAGE. NO POPULATION RANGES OR CLINICAL ZONES FROZEN.**

## Purpose

The Neurostability Atlas is an independent coordinate/evidence resource organized around the locked NSD System-Model objects. It is not a lookup table of desired Engine answers and may not be used to tune the Structural Engine toward expected biological, clinical, historical or chi values.

Atlas construction proceeds in this order:

1. define coordinate ontology;
2. define derivation/licensing requirements;
3. define source/provenance and pathway-specific independence requirements;
4. inventory candidate evidence sources;
5. extract native coordinates and context;
6. map Function and Limit regions;
7. only after sufficient evidence, consider bounded empirical reference ranges or labels.

## Coordinate families

### A. Native spectral / pole coordinates

These are the highest-priority Atlas coordinates because they are closest to the identified local generator and require the fewest interpretive steps.

Candidate fields, where supported:

- continuous-time pole real part `sigma_j` [1/s];
- angular frequency `omega_j` [rad/s];
- frequency `f_j = omega_j/(2*pi)` [Hz];
- discrete-time eigenvalue where that is the source-native object;
- decay time `tau_j = -1/sigma_j` for stable modes, with derivation explicit;
- stability class: stable / unstable / non-oscillatory / unresolved;
- mode persistence across adjacent windows/conditions when independently supportable;
- pole uncertainty or interval/covariance descriptor when estimator-native uncertainty is available.

These coordinates may be stored without any chi interpretation.

### B. Modal / carrier coordinates

Candidate fields:

- channel-resolved carrier/mode vector or normalized representation;
- carrier/subspace similarity between conditions/windows;
- individual-mode assignment margin;
- invariant/crowded-subspace similarity;
- mode/cluster membership where identifiable;
- channel participation/loading/leverage descriptors;
- carrier uncertainty or identifiability state;
- anatomical/channel metadata kept distinct from mechanistic interpretation.

Individual carrier and subspace coordinates remain separate.

### C. Conglomerate / system-organization coordinates

Candidate fields:

- local observable order/rank;
- whole supported observable-subspace dimension;
- number of individually resolvable modes;
- number/dimension of crowded clusters/subspaces;
- added/lost/shared observable structure across perturbation/condition;
- participation-distribution summaries;
- relational spectral geometry among resolvable mode/cluster objects;
- estimator agreement/disagreement pattern;
- system-level epistemic state (`ADMIT`, `PARTIAL`, `REFUSE`, `UNRESOLVED`, `INDETERMINATE`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`) with component reasons preserved.

No arithmetic aggregate of these objects is automatically a valid system scalar.

### D. Crowding / identifiability coordinates

Candidate fields include, where all inputs are valid:

- pair/cluster frequency separation;
- separation normalized by decay width, e.g. `|omega_i-omega_j|/(alpha_i+alpha_j)` as a method/native crowding descriptor;
- individual assignment margin;
- individual carrier similarity;
- joint subspace similarity;
- observable-rank stability;
- ambiguity class: individually identifiable / subspace-only identifiable / unresolved.

No universal crowding cutoff is frozen.

### E. Model-adequacy coordinates

Keep separate rather than combine into one score:

- residual/model-error temporal-structure descriptor;
- out-of-sample covariance reconstruction error;
- out-of-sample prediction/reconstruction error;
- fitted stability/admissibility;
- order sensitivity;
- window sensitivity;
- method exceptions/refusals;
- adequacy interpretation state at the specific claim level.

A coordinate generated by a generic output predictor must not be relabeled as an SSI innovation statistic.

### F. Uncertainty / identifiability coordinates

Candidate fields:

- estimator-native pole/frequency/decay uncertainty;
- carrier/subspace uncertainty;
- order/rank uncertainty or competing admissible orders;
- assignment ambiguity;
- crowding-induced individual-vs-subspace ambiguity;
- calibration provenance;
- marginal versus simultaneous uncertainty convention when eventually qualified;
- `INDETERMINATE` reasons.

Current NSD-native Hankel uncertainty work is developmental and does not yet establish an Atlas-ready modal interval coordinate.

### G. Functional / perturbation context coordinates

Every Atlas row/record should be interpretable in context, including where available:

- recording state/task/condition;
- perturbation/intervention/exposure;
- pre/during/post or equivalent temporal relation;
- anatomical/channel montage context;
- sampling rate and preprocessing that can alter coordinate meaning;
- recording duration/window policy;
- subject/system class and inclusion criteria;
- research role: `NOMINAL_FUNCTION`, `PERTURBED_FUNCTION`, `BOUNDARY_OR_TRANSITION`, `RARE_NATURAL_LIMIT`;
- Function/Limit state for the specific coordinate claim: `WORKS_HERE`, `STOPS_WORKING_HERE`, `NOT_KNOWN_HERE`.

## Chi firewall

`chi` is **not an Atlas base coordinate**.

A chi-like coordinate may be admitted only if all of the following are true:

1. the source/native model supports the required second-order factorization or equivalent physically justified parameterization;
2. the transformation is explicitly derived;
3. units and sign conventions are preserved;
4. the transformation has an epistemic/provenance status;
5. the coordinate is not being created solely to force compatibility with historical SymC values;
6. its use does not replace the underlying native pole/modal/system coordinates.

Generic complex poles do not automatically license `chi = gamma/(2|omega|)` or any related ratio.

## Atlas evidence record requirements

Every coordinate record must carry at minimum:

- `atlas_record_id`;
- coordinate family and name;
- native/derived status;
- numerical value or structured object;
- units;
- source identity/version;
- extraction location/table/figure/text status;
- estimator/method used by the source;
- population/system/condition context;
- preprocessing/window context where material;
- uncertainty/identifiability status;
- evidence tier;
- research role;
- Function/Limit state where applicable;
- pathway-specific independence grades;
- derivation record if derived;
- scope ceiling;
- explicit nonclaims/limitations where needed.

## Evidence tiers

Initial Atlas evidence tiers:

- `DIRECT_NUMERIC_NATIVE`: native coordinate numerically reported or reproducibly extractable from source data/code;
- `REPRODUCED_FROM_SOURCE_DATA`: coordinate independently reproduced from accessible source data with frozen code/provenance;
- `DERIVED_LICENSED`: derived from source-native quantities by an explicitly licensed transformation;
- `SNIPPET_OR_PARTIAL`: only partial source verification; not sufficient for decisive Atlas calibration;
- `BLOCKED_SOURCE`: source exists but decisive value/method cannot be verified;
- `INELIGIBLE`: fails provenance, method, independence, or coordinate-admission requirements.

Figure-only extraction should be treated as lower-evidence unless the protocol for that coordinate explicitly licenses it.

## Independence pathways

For each decisive Atlas use, record separately:

- data independence;
- cohort/system independence;
- outcome/label independence;
- parameter/tuning independence;
- method/estimator independence;
- Atlas independence;
- source/literature independence;
- temporal/decisive-evidence independence.

No overall independence label may hide a forcing dependency on the pathway relevant to the claim.

## Initial Atlas mapping priority

### Priority 1: Native coordinates

1. frequency/pole/decay coordinates;
2. observable order/rank and mode count where methodologically meaningful;
3. carrier/subspace and participation structure;
4. perturbation-dependent changes in the above.

### Priority 2: Limit/identifiability coordinates

5. crowding/assignment/subspace transition evidence;
6. model-adequacy and refusal evidence;
7. uncertainty/calibration evidence.

### Priority 3: Derived coordinates

8. only explicitly licensed derived coordinates;
9. chi only after a separate admission/derivation record is satisfied.

## First Atlas source classes to inventory

The initial source inventory should deliberately seek both functioning and limit behavior across:

- healthy/resting neural recordings;
- task or sensory perturbations;
- sleep/arousal/state transitions;
- pharmacological or stimulation perturbations where methodologically usable;
- seizure/epileptiform or other strong dynamical transitions as potential boundary/rare-natural testbeds, subject to independent selection and confound checks;
- neurodegenerative/neuropsychiatric cohorts only after coordinate extraction rules are frozen enough to prevent phenotype-driven Engine tuning.

These are source classes, not claims that all are equally suitable or required.

## Current stop conditions

Do not freeze empirical reference zones until:

- coordinate definitions are stable;
- extraction provenance is auditable;
- enough sources exist to characterize method/context dependence;
- known method effects are not being mistaken for biological coordinates;
- success-seeking and failure-seeking source bias have been checked;
- independence pathways are recorded;
- any derived coordinate has passed its transformation/admission gate.

## Disposition

**Atlas coordinate ontology v0.1 is ready for source inventory and coordinate extraction.**

The next computational/research object is a machine-readable coordinate registry plus source/evidence ledger so candidate Atlas entries can be accumulated without changing the locked System Model or back-driving Engine rules.
